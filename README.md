Reflective Analysis: My map explores how the UBC campus has changed over the past 100 years and how it is expected to develop in the future. It highlights significant developments in the built environment as well as proposed projects such as the SkyTrain extension and new campus infrastructure. I designed this map primarily for UBC students and faculty, providing an interactive way to explore the history of a place where they spend a significant amount of their time and consider how it may continue to change. I think my map successfully communicates the scale of physical development across campus through connecting historical imagery with present day locations. However, by focusing on infrastructure, the map does not show aspects of social and cultural changes that have occurred alongside the physical development of the campus. For example, questions about how much the student population has increased, and how campus diversity and culture have changed remain unanswered.

My design process began with searching for historical aerial imagery that would allow viewers to compare the campus across different time periods. However, I struggled to find imagery dating back to UBC's earliest development that could be incorporated directly into my map. Instead, I used historical aerial photos to illustrate changes to the campus over time. I also wanted viewers to engage with specific locations across campus, so I created a GeoJSON file identifying 3 major campus developments. I added pop-ups to these locations with historical images, allowing viewers to see what the area looked like before development and changes. Lastly, I wanted to add a component that displayed the Future of UBC, so I decided to incorporate the proposed SkyTrain extension with a line layer I created in geojson.io alongside UBC’s Future Buildings GIS dataset.

I chose to present my map as a story map to make the map more interactive and guide viewers through UBC’s development over time. I think this format is particularly useful with the theme of my map because it allows viewers to scroll through different map layers and historical periods that build on one another. At the same time, the ability to pan around campus and click on different development locations encourages users to connect historical information with their own spatial understanding of UBC. I do, however, recognize that the choices of what developments are displayed on the map are my choice and may not be seen as significant by viewers. By highlighting major infrastructure projects, the map could suggest that campus development is based solely off of physical expansion. One improvement would be to incorporate information about population growth, cultural changes, and the people affected by campus development. I had also originally hoped to create an interactive slider that would allow viewers to compare historical aerial imagery directly with the present-day campus. Although I was unable to implement this, I think it would have added an additional element of interactivity.
